Why it matters

Heritage is not what survives.
It is what someone decides to keep.

Egypt's official monuments are only a fraction of the country's heritage. Around them are thousands of places that matter to the people who live near them: the workshop where a craft survived five centuries, the station clock still set to the last departure, the village that painted its drowned home back onto its walls. These places usually exist only in memory — and memory, unlike stone, is not protected.

Kenzory exists to write those memories down, keep documented history separate from memory, and let a community of reviewers confirm what is true. One record can be enough to keep a place from being forgotten.

How verification works

  • A record is submitted with sources and photos.
  • Two community reviewers check the claims against references.
  • A field visit or reliable local testimony confirms the site exists.
  • Documented history is kept separate from personal accounts.
  • The verified badge is added; the record joins the map.
